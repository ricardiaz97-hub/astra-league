# Astra League Character Design Bible

## Purpose

This guide records the design language already present in the character sheets. It is a continuity guide for making new illustrations, poses, expressions, and game art that still read as the same cast. The sheets are the primary visual reference; this document describes them rather than replacing them with a new direction.

## The central idea

**A recognizable real person, reimagined as a confident member of one stylish team.** The cast feels contemporary and approachable, with the polish, expressive faces, strong color, and crisp finish of anime game illustration. Their shared team kit makes them belong together; their real identifying features, personal expression, and accent color make each one distinct.

The design is a blend of grounded contemporary streetwear and heightened anime presentation. It is not medieval fantasy costume. Keep the recognizable modern clothing, practical pockets and straps, sneakers, and personal accessories visible even when the pose or lighting becomes dramatic.

## What holds the cast together

### Shared uniform language

- A cropped cream or warm-white jacket with a dark charcoal/black hood, collar, or inner layer.
- Dark utility trousers, generally relaxed and tapered or gathered at the ankle, with cargo pockets and light side stripes or panels.
- Chunky cream-and-dark sneakers, with accent-color details where appropriate.
- Functional-looking straps, buckles, wrist accessories, tabs, and pockets. They create a sporty, technical team identity; they should remain readable and not become random decoration.
- A four-point star mark used as the team emblem. It appears on clothing and accessories and may be adapted to a character's accent color.

These are common ingredients, not a rigid identical outfit. The sheets already show variations in tops, sleeve length, hood and strap arrangement, gloves, jewelry, instruments, and other personal details. Preserve those established differences when drawing a specific person.

### Team palette and individual accents

Cream, charcoal, and black form the shared base. Each character sheet uses an accent color across selected jacket details, tabs, emblem, accessories, and graphic sheet elements. The accent is a quick recognition cue, not a requirement to recolor the whole outfit.

The current art context records these assigned accents: orange for Armando, Cris, Isma, Mau, Tefa, Speaker, Yesy, Gaby, and Andrés; pink for Rebecca; purple for Connie; green for Diego; gray for Julio; yellow for Jenny. Keep these assignments when those characters are depicted. Other supplied sheets, including Fiorella, Sharon, Ricardo, Milena, and the sheet named `p`, are also part of the visual reference set; use their own visible sheet colors and details rather than guessing a new assignment.

### The star motif

The four-point star is the cast's clearest shared graphic. It signals team membership and ties together the jacket, cargo details, and sheet design. Treat it as a consistent emblem: preserve its four-point silhouette and placement where visible in the reference. It can take on the character accent color. Do not replace it with a different symbol or add unrelated competing insignias.

## Keep each person recognizable

The sheets are based on specific people. Likeness and individual identity take priority over generic anime prettiness or uniformity. Preserve the distinguishing features visible in the reference: face shape, skin tone, hairline and hairstyle, facial hair, glasses, earrings, build, and characteristic expression. Keep personal items and performance roles where shown—for example, microphones, instruments, or audio gear.

Anime stylization should clarify these features, not erase them. Faces can be clean and expressive, with simplified details and deliberate eyes and brows, while retaining the person's recognizable proportions. Do not give everyone the same face, eye shape, body type, hairstyle, or default smile. Do not combine separate people into one character.

Expression and pose are part of characterization. Several sheets show a range of expressions and active performance poses. Use that range: confident, joyful, calm, focused, or mid-performance as appropriate to the person and scene. Avoid making every portrait the same open-mouth cheer.

Respect established character-specific facts in the art context. Julio's eyes are gently closed because he is blind. Mau and Yesy are separate people. Andrés is the lead singer and the same person in all three reference photos. Preserve these distinctions consistently.

## Shape, detail, and readability

Silhouettes are relaxed, sporty, and practical: cropped outerwear above roomy utility pants and substantial sneakers. Keep the jacket, hood/collar, trouser volume, pockets, straps, and shoes legible at full-body scale. Avoid piling on armor, capes, fantasy robes, oversized weapons, or extra accessories that obscure the established streetwear silhouette.

Detail is concentrated in useful places: seams and folds, pockets, buckles, straps, patches, footwear, jewelry, and character props. Use larger clear shapes first and small accents second. The supplied sheets use close-up panels to show important design details; new details should likewise reinforce identity or team membership rather than add noise.

## Rendering and sheet presentation

The established finish is polished anime-inspired illustration with confident dark outlines, clean shape separation, warm skin and fabric lighting, and cel-shaded or softly blended shadow planes. Highlights and folds are graphic and deliberate. Keep materials distinguishable—cloth, metal hardware, hair, and skin—without pushing toward photorealism.

Character sheets use a warm cream background, full-body views, close-up detail panels, and expressive head studies. Accent-colored angular graphics frame the character and echo their color. Maintain clear spacing and a light background so the dark outfit and silhouette read cleanly. For an in-world scene or game portrait, the layout can change; the character design itself should remain faithful.

## Using anime and HoYoverse influence

Use the broad qualities the project owner named: expressive anime character acting, clear silhouettes, vivid but controlled color, polished illustration, and dramatic light. The supplied sheets set the actual character-specific rules. Apply the influence through presentation and rendering while retaining these people, their contemporary teamwear, their star emblem, and their individual accent treatments. Do not turn them into lookalikes of existing game characters or replace their designs with a generic fantasy roster.

## Practical checklist for new character art

Before considering an illustration finished, check:

- Does this still look like the specific person in their sheet?
- Are their established hair, face, skin tone, body type, accessories, and role respected?
- Does the shared cream/charcoal team language remain visible?
- Is their established accent color used in the same supporting role?
- Is the four-point star consistent with the references?
- Does the pose or expression fit the character instead of repeating a cast-wide template?
- Are the clothes and identifying details readable at the image's intended size?
- Does the anime polish support the existing design rather than redesign it?
